**Michael Levin** — biologist at **Tufts University** (Allen Discovery Center) and the **Wyss Institute, Harvard**; known for work on **morphogenesis, bioelectricity, and "basal cognition"** — how cells and tissues store/process information and compute target morphologies independent of the genome. Co-author of both [Xenobots](xenobots.md) papers.

## Role in this wiki
- **Co-author, [Kriegman et al. 2020](../sources/kriegman-2020-reconfigurable-organisms.md) & [2021](../sources/kriegman-2021-kinematic-self-replication.md)** — his lab's understanding of cellular **developmental plasticity** is what makes reconfigurable organisms possible: liberated frog cells **self-organize into stable forms and behaviors** never exhibited by the source animal, with no genetic editing.
- His framing — that **biological matter harbors latent forms and "intelligence" reachable without selection or genetic engineering** — is the biological echo of the wiki's recurring **emergence** theme ([artificial life](../concepts/alife/artificial-life-and-self-replication.md)), and a complement to the computational-emergence view of [Blaise Agüera y Arcas](blaise-aguera-y-arcas.md).
