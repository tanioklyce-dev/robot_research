**Douglas Hofstadter** — cognitive scientist; Distinguished Professor at Indiana University Bloomington, where he directs the Center for Research on Concepts and Cognition. Author of *Gödel, Escher, Bach: an Eternal Golden Braid* (1979, Pulitzer Prize) and *I Am a Strange Loop* (2007). In this wiki he appears as the origin of the **[strange loop](../concepts/agents/strange-loops-and-self-reference.md)** — the self-closing hierarchy that both ingested sources use as their frame for AI agency and machine selfhood.

## The idea the wiki uses

A **strange loop** is a hierarchy that returns to its starting level when you ascend it — the observer becomes part of the observed. Hofstadter's thesis is that the human "I" *is* one: a symbolic self-model that represents and thereby modulates the substrate producing it, exerting causation "at the appropriate level of abstraction."

The quotation both sources reach for:

> *"In the end, we are self-perceiving, self-inventing, locked-in mirages that are little miracles of self-reference."*

## Position in the wiki

Hofstadter is a **cited authority, not an ingested author** — neither *GEB* nor *I Am a Strange Loop* is in `raw/`, and both wiki sources that invoke him are secondary ([Carroll's deck](../sources/arcnem-strange-loops-ai-agents.md), [Masood's article](../sources/masood-hofstadter-strange-loop-consciousness.md)). Treat claims attributed to him here as reported at one remove.

Worth noting for context that the wiki does not source: Hofstadter's own long-running research program is on **analogy as the core of cognition** (Copycat, fluid concepts), and he has been publicly skeptical of large language models as a route to understanding — a stance neither ingested source engages while borrowing his central metaphor.
