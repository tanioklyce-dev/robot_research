**Stephen Wolfram** — physicist, computer scientist, and founder of Wolfram Research (Mathematica, Wolfram|Alpha, the Wolfram Language). In this wiki he is the analyst of [cellular automata](../concepts/alife/cellular-automata.md) and the originator of several ideas the ALife/emergence branch leans on.

## Key ideas (as they appear in the wiki)
- **Elementary cellular automata + Rule 30.** Systematic study of 1D binary CAs; **Rule 30** produces apparent randomness from a trivial rule — his canonical example of **computational irreducibility**.
- **A New Kind of Science (2002)** — argues simple computational rules (CAs above all) are fundamental models of natural complexity; introduces the **four behavior classes** (the [Game of Life](game-of-life.md) is Class 4).
- **Computational irreducibility** — for many systems there is no shortcut to the outcome but to run the computation. See [cellular automata](../concepts/alife/cellular-automata.md).
- **Principle of Computational Equivalence** — almost all systems above a low threshold are equivalently computationally powerful (hence universality and undecidability are generic).
- **Ruliology / the Ruliad** — the science of what rules naturally do, and the space of all possible computations (his Wolfram Physics Project).

## In this wiki
- His [2025 essay on Game-of-Life engineering](../sources/wolfram-2025-game-of-life-engineering.md) reframes 50 years of CA pattern-discovery as a study of **innovation** ("metaengineering"): **construction vs. search**, the modularity/comprehensibility trade-off, and irreducible computation as a "spark" to be "caged." It connects to the wiki's recurring **designed/comprehensible vs mined/emergent** tension across [JEPA](../concepts/world-models/jepa.md), [Computational Life](../sources/computational-life-self-replicating-programs-paper.md), and the [local-rules synthesis](../syntheses/alife/local-rules-global-complexity.md).
