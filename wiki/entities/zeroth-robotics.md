**Zeroth Robotics** — consumer- and light-commercial robotics company selling a three-product line of home and outdoor robots direct to buyers, launched into the US market around **CES 2026**.

> [!note] Confidence is low on everything except the storefront
> The wiki holds exactly one Zeroth source: the [M1 product page](../sources/zeroth-m1-product-page.md). Company facts below are **secondary-sourced from CES-launch coverage and Zeroth's own site news headlines**, not from a company primary — Zeroth's "About Us," "Our Identity," "Our Journey," "Our Innovation" and "Press" pages all return **HTTP 404**. Per this wiki's [primary-source convention](../../CLAUDE.md), treat funding, corporate structure and the AI stack as unverified.

## Company claims (secondary, unverified)

- Reported as a **Chinese company connected to Suzhou JoyIn Intelligent Technology Co.**, emerging from stealth and entering the US market in **January 2026**.
- The site's own news headlines state: **"Zeroth Secures Three Angel Rounds Totaling US$70M, Valued at US$351M Within a Year of Inception"** (dated 2025-10-25), a **brand launch and CES debut** planned for January 2026 (2025-11-25), and an appearance at "Bridge Summit" with W1 (2025-10-30). The linked articles behind these headlines were not retrieved.
- CES coverage reports the [M1](zeroth-m1.md) **runs Google Gemini** for conversation, reminders and fall/emergency recognition. **No Zeroth page mentions Gemini**, cloud services, or connectivity at all.
- Coverage variously cites M1 starting prices of **$2,499** and **$2,899**.

## Assessment

What Zeroth contributes to this wiki is a **price point, not a capability**. The in-home assistive cluster here is anchored on [Stretch](stretch.md) (**$29,950**) and [Kinova Jaco](kinova-jaco.md) (~$35k), with the strongest household results coming from lab systems ([OK-Robot](ok-robot.md), [Robot Utility Models](robot-utility-models.md)). A consumer device advertising fall detection and daily assistance at **$2,499** tests a floor those platforms have never approached.

Against that, the storefront substantiates none of it — no compute spec, no privacy statement, no SDK behind the "open platform" positioning, no ship date, and no independent review or teardown found. The nearest comparison in the wiki is not Stretch but the **social/companion** tier: expressive presence and monitoring rather than physical assistance. Nothing on the M1 page describes it lifting, carrying, or manipulating anything.
