**Kawin Ethayarajh** — Assistant Professor of **Applied AI** at the University of Chicago Booth School of Business; co-organizer of the [World Modeling Workshop](../sources/chicago-booth-world-modeling-workshop-2026.md). Background in computational linguistics and NLP (*"I'm old enough that when I started doing computational linguistics… a very big thing was part-of-speech tagging"*).

In this wiki he is the **historian of the term**. His [Day 3 closing remarks](../sources/chicago-booth-world-modeling-workshop-2026-day3.md) do what three days of keynotes did not: rather than proposing another definition of "world model," he shows the phrase has meant **three different things over 80 years**, and that the disagreement in the room is a residue of that.

## The three-era taxonomy

| Period | "World model" meant |
|---|---|
| **1940s–1980s** | A **symbolic description** of the world, typically **specified by hand** |
| **1980s–2010s** | A **probabilistic model** of how the world changes |
| **2012–** | A **deep network** predicting observations, consequences, and/or affinities between states |

with [LeCun](yann-lecun.md) holding a narrower fourth position — a neural world model that is **separate from any actor in that world**. See [world model](../concepts/world-models/world-model.md), where the full periodization now lives.

The prehistory he reaches for is the part nothing else in this wiki carries: **Epictetus (c. 125 CE)** on rehearsing the rowdy bathhouse in advance; **Tolman's rats** taking the sealed-off maze's direct route 38% of the time, having built a cognitive map rather than a policy; **Winograd's SHRDLU (1970)**; **Cyc**; **Pearl**; **Sutton's Dyna**.

His verdict on SHRDLU is the sentence to keep, because it is aimed at the present:

> *"Much in the way of LLM psychosis nowadays, people were really amazed by this program… they thought we were so close to solving understanding with natural language, because if we can do this in this [toy] space then clearly we just need to scale this up a little bit and it's totally solved. Turns out this was kind of a dead end — but it was still a very important milestone."*

## The fork he draws through the deep-learning era

Post-2012 he splits the field into **scaling language** (humans have a world model, it is crystallized in language, predict the next token) and **scaling experience** (you have to go out into the world or a simulation of it — MuZero, Tesla Autopilot, XLand). Today's synthesis occupies all four cells: real/synthetic × language/experience, with **humanoid robots in homes** named as the real-experience collection strategy, *"as slightly terrifying as that might be."* Over the top of all four he places **Amodei's "big blob of compute"** — the position that the source of the data is secondary to growing the compute.

> [!warning] One claim in the remarks the wiki cannot source
> That **DeepMind's bet on scaling experience is part of why Google fell behind on language models.** Stated as fact in a closing summary; plausible and widely repeated, but nothing in this wiki supports it. Flagged rather than adopted.

## The three open questions he leaves

1. **Explicit vs implicit** — is an implicitly-learned language world model sufficient, or is an explicit one necessary? *"Could we resolve this using theory?"*
2. **Latent vs observation space** — LeCun's position, explicitly still contested.
3. **Adaptation** — *"the world is constantly changing, and moreover, as we release more and more AI into the wild, the world is going to adapt in response to the rise of AI."*

The third is the one that reaches furthest. Day 2 met reflexivity as a **finance** problem — the modelled system changes because it is modelled. Ethayarajh generalizes it: as AI drives more activity, **every domain starts to acquire the property that made finance hard**. On that reading the wiki's [world models for financial markets](../syntheses/society/world-models-for-financial-markets.md) synthesis is an early case study, not a special case.
