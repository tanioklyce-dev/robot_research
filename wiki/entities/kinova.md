**Kinova** — Canadian robotics company (Boisbriand, Quebec, founded 2006) that makes the [Jaco](kinova-jaco.md) assistive robotic arm and a widely-used line of research manipulators (Gen2/Gen3). The rare company that spans both sides of the wiki's assistive-robotics coverage: a *commercial, insurance-reimbursed medical device* for wheelchair users and the *academic research arm* on which systems like the [Nanavati feeding robot](../sources/nanavati2025-feeding-out-of-lab.md) are built.

## History

- Founded **2006** by **Charles Deguire** and Louis-Joseph Caron L'Écuyer. Origin story: Deguire's uncle Jacques "Jaco" Forest, living with muscular dystrophy (d. 1993), built himself a makeshift mechanical manipulation aid; three of Deguire's uncles had the disease ([Jaco source page](../sources/kinova-jaco-assistive-arm.md)).
- Wheelchair-mountable arm demonstrated ~2009; **Jaco commercial launch 2010**; first customer in the Netherlands ([Jaco source page](../sources/kinova-jaco-assistive-arm.md)).
- Expanded with offices in Germany and China 2016–2018 ([Jaco source page](../sources/kinova-jaco-assistive-arm.md)).

## Products

- **[Jaco assistive arm](kinova-jaco.md)** — the flagship: wheelchair-mounted, ~US$35k, marketed as a medical device, sold primarily into markets with insurance reimbursement codes (Germany, Netherlands).
- **Research manipulators** — Jaco Gen2 and the Gen3 line; standard academic hardware for assistive-manipulation research. In the wiki: the [feeding system](../sources/nanavati2025-feeding-out-of-lab.md) deployed in-home by Nanavati et al. runs on a Jaco Gen2 6-DOF (~$50k research configuration).

## Business model note

Kinova's stated strategy is to concentrate on jurisdictions where the arm is **reimbursable through health-insurance codes** rather than out-of-pocket — the clearest wiki example of reimbursement policy, not technology, gating assistive-robot deployment ([Jaco source page](../sources/kinova-jaco-assistive-arm.md)). Contrast with [Hello Robot](hello-robot.md), which priced [Stretch](stretch.md) for research labs instead.
