**Belief state** — when the world's state is not directly observed, the thing an agent can actually carry forward is a **distribution over states** consistent with everything seen so far. In hidden-Markov theory the same object is called a **mixed state**; in POMDPs, the belief; in computational mechanics, a **predictive feature** or causal state. It is the sufficient statistic of the past for predicting the future, and it is the honest answer to "what is the state of a world model?"

The wiki reaches this idea from two directions that never cite each other: the [World Labs functional taxonomy](../../sources/world-labs-functional-taxonomy.md) derives renderer / simulator / planner as "the three outputs of the POMDP loop," and every latent world model here — [JEPA](jepa.md), [DINO-WM](../../entities/dino-wm.md), [PLDM](../../entities/pldm.md) — learns a vector that is a belief state in all but name.

## The classical result, and why it is uncomfortable

**Blackwell (1957)** analyzed the entropy of *functions of finite-state Markov chains* — i.e. hidden Markov chains — and established two things that a modern latent world model has to live with ([as transcribed by Jurgens & Crutchfield](../../sources/jurgens-crutchfield-hmp-entropy-rate.md)):

1. **Unifilarity is the dividing line.** If the current state plus the next observed symbol determines the next state (*unifilar*), Shannon's entropy-rate formula applies directly. Drop that property and **there is no closed-form expression for the entropy rate**.
2. **The sufficient statistic is generically infinite.** Predicting a nonunifilar HMC requires an uncountable set of causal states. A process can be generated by a *finite* machine and still be predictable by **no finite unifilar machine**.

Blackwell's constructive substitute replaces the finite state average in Shannon's formula with an integral over the mixed-state simplex against a stationary distribution — now called the **Blackwell measure**:

`H = −∫ Σₐ rₐ(w) log rₐ(w) dQ(w)`

Same shape as Shannon's formula, uncountable index set. Jurgens & Crutchfield later showed the mixed-state dynamic is an **iterated function system**, so the integral is computable as a time average along one long orbit — the quantity is calculable even though no closed form exists.

> [!warning] The uncomfortable consequence for learned world models
> Every latent world model in this wiki compresses history into a **fixed-width vector**. Blackwell's result says that for a nonunifilar process the sufficient statistic is generically **infinite-dimensional**. So a finite latent is provably lossy in a way that is *not* a training failure and will not be fixed by scale — the question is only whether the loss bites at realistic horizons. Nobody in this wiki has asked. It is the sharpest available theoretical statement of why [world models degrade over long rollouts](world-model-evaluation.md), a phenomenon [WorldRoamBench](../../entities/worldroambench.md) measures empirically and attributes to drift.

## The discrete cousin: Myhill-Nerode equivalence

[Vafa et al. (2024)](../../sources/vafa-world-model-implicit.md) attack the same object from the deterministic side. If the world is a **DFA**, the Myhill-Nerode theorem says its states are exactly the equivalence classes of prefixes admitting the same set of valid suffixes — the discrete, observable-alphabet version of "the belief state is the sufficient statistic of the past."

That makes world-model recovery testable without probing internals:

| | Question | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| **Sequence compression** | Do two prefixes reaching the same state admit the same continuations? | model **over**-distinguishes |
| **Sequence distinction** | Are two prefixes reaching different states separated by some valid suffix? | model **under**-distinguishes |

And the answer for current models is bad in a specific way: a transformer that produces the true shortest path through Manhattan **97%** of the time has a compression precision of **0.10**, and its reconstructed map contains streets with impossible orientations and flyovers. GPT-4 solves seating-arrangement logic puzzles with task accuracy **1.00** and compression precision **0.21**.

**Task success and belief-state coherence are different variables.** That is the same lesson [stable-worldmodel](../../sources/stable-worldmodel-paper.md) reaches by finding prediction MSE a poor proxy for planning success, and [WorldArena](../../entities/worldarena.md) reaches with r = 0.360 between perceptual score and action planning. Three formalisms, one finding.

## Why coverage beats realism

Vafa et al.'s structural result, replicated across navigation and Othello: **models trained on random or synthetic traversals recover more world-model structure than models trained on real, expert data.** Real data is shortest paths and championship games — it never visits the state space broadly enough for the model to learn which distinct prefixes land in the same state.

This is a live challenge to the teleoperation orthodoxy. [Mobile ALOHA](../../sources/mobile-aloha-paper.md), [DROID](../../entities/droid.md), and [Figure's Index](../../entities/figure-index.md) all collect *expert demonstrations* — structurally the shortest-paths regime that scored 0.10 here. No VLA in this wiki has been given a compression-style test.

## The field is arguing about this without invoking the theorem

At the **third World Modeling Workshop** ([Chicago Booth, Aug 2026](../../sources/chicago-booth-world-modeling-workshop-2026.md)) the opening keynote was on *Blackwell's world-models theorem* and the closing panel argued over exactly the object Blackwell formalized — without the two being connected.

[Danijar Hafner](../../entities/danijar-hafner.md) reported a negative empirical result: *"I tried this maybe five years ago, really hard, to have a representation that contains the entire belief of states you could be in. It just worked so much better empirically to have a probabilistic model you can sample from."* [Yann LeCun](../../entities/yann-lecun.md) disagreed on the remedy but not the diagnosis: *"the best way to handle uncertainty is to just eliminate the information you can't predict — that's the main argument of JEPA."*

**Blackwell's result is the theory under both positions.** If the sufficient statistic is generically infinite-dimensional, then explicitly representing the belief should fail at scale (Hafner's finding), and deliberately discarding unpredictable information is a principled response rather than a heuristic (LeCun's argument). Sampling and JEPA-style discarding are two different ways of not paying for an object that provably will not fit.
