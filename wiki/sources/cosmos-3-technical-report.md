# Cosmos 3: Omnimodal World Models for Physical AI

## Summary

Cosmos 3 is NVIDIA's family of **omnimodal world models** that jointly process and generate **language, image, video, audio, and action** within a single **Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)** architecture. Its thesis is that the separation of Physical AI into distinct model classes — VLMs for perception, video generators / forward-dynamics models for simulation, and VLAs / world-action models for control — is "fundamentally limiting," because understanding requires reasoning about future evolution and generation requires a compact world representation. A single network handles all of them: depending on input→output configuration the *same* model acts as a VLM, a text-to-image / text-to-video / image-to-video generator, an audio-visual generator, a forward-dynamics model, an inverse-dynamics model, or a joint video-action **policy**. NVIDIA reports it as state-of-the-art across a broad understanding+generation suite, the **#1 open-weight Text-to-Image and Image-to-Video model (Artificial Analysis leaderboard, 2026-05-28)** and the **#1 policy model on RoboArena's real-world benchmark (2026-05-30)** at the time of writing. Everything (code, checkpoints, curated synthetic datasets, eval benchmark) is released under the **OpenMDW-1.1** license.

This is a generational leap from the prior Cosmos-Predict / Cosmos-Reason / Cosmos-Transfer / Cosmos-Policy line (see [NVIDIA Cosmos](../entities/nvidia-cosmos.md)): those were separate models; Cosmos 3 subsumes them into one omni-model.

## Model architecture

- **Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT), dual-tower.** Each transformer decoder layer carries **two independent parameter sets** — a **reasoner** tower (AR subsequence) and a **generator** tower (diffusion subsequence) — each with its own LayerNorms, attention projections, and FFN. Both towers are **initialized from a pre-trained Qwen3-VL VLM** (Nano from Qwen3-VL-8B, Super from Qwen3-VL-32B), inheriting language + visual reasoning.
- **Two subsequences in one token stream.** An **autoregressive (AR)** subsequence (language + ViT-encoded vision tokens) is generated by next-token prediction; a **diffusion (DM)** subsequence (VAE video/image tokens + audio + action tokens) is generated by iterative denoising (**flow-matching velocity objective**).
- **Dual-stream joint attention.** AR tokens attend *causally* over AR only; DM tokens attend *bidirectionally* over `[AR; DM]`. AR is never updated from DM tokens, preserving causal integrity — diffusion is conditioned on the AR context while reasoning stays autoregressively self-contained.
- **Encoders.** Vision: a vision-language-aligned **ViT** (16×16 patch, DeepStack aggregation, interleaved text timestamps) for understanding, jointly trained; a **frozen Wan2.2-TI2V-5B video VAE** (4× temporal, 32×32 spatial compression) for generation. Audio: a **frozen audio VAE** (48 kHz stereo, 25 tokens/sec). **Action is a first-class modality** with its own tokens.
- **Unified action representation (Fig. 3).** Heterogeneous embodiments map into compact vectors from shared geometric components: **ego pose (9D = 3D translation + 6D rotation)**, **effector pose (9D)**, and **grasp state** (1D gripper open/close, or 15D = 3D × 5 fingertips). Totals: autonomous vehicle 9D, camera motion 9D, single-arm robot 10D, dual-arm robot 20D, humanoid 29D, egocentric motion 57D. Poses are **relative-transform pseudo-actions** (ΔT = T⁻¹ₜ₋₁Tₜ) so the model avoids embodiment-specific PID/actuation details. **Domain-aware input/output projection matrices** per embodiment, shared MoT backbone; 6D rotations decoded back to SO(3) via SVD.
- **3D MRoPE with absolute temporal modulation** aligns video/audio/action tokens on a shared physical time axis (TPS_base = 24/4 = 6).
- **Action generation modes (Fig. 4):** *forward dynamics* (denoise video given clean actions), *inverse dynamics* (denoise actions given clean video), *policy* (denoise both jointly — predicts the intervention **and** its expected visual consequence).

## Key claims (with numbers)

**Reasoner (48 benchmarks, 4 categories — Table 10):**
- **General avg.** Super 73.7, Nano 69.6 — competitive with Qwen3-VL-32B (72.8) but trails closed **Gemini 3.1 Pro (77.5)**.
- **Robotics avg.** Super **57.8**, Nano 55.1 — beats Qwen3-VL-32B (52.6), Cosmos-Reason2-32B (55.0), RynnBrain, MiMo-Embodied; small gap to Gemini 3.1 Pro (58.2).
- **Smart-infrastructure avg.** Super **62.6**, Nano 61.0 — beats Gemini 3.1 Pro (58.6).
- **Driving avg.** Super **79.3**, Nano 76.0 — far ahead of Gemini 3.1 Pro (47.2) and all open baselines.

**Image generation (Table 11, UniGenBench "All"/1170 prompts):**
- **Cosmos3-Super-Text2Image 91.36** — beats Gemini 3 Pro Image (90.69, closed), FLUX.2-dev (87.60), Qwen-Image-2512 (84.25). **#1 open-weight, #4 overall** on the Artificial Analysis T2I leaderboard (crowdsourced voting, 2026-05-28).

**Video generation (Table 1 overview):** Super Text2Video 80.0, Image2Video 82.8 (best open-source per Artificial Analysis); competitive with closed Veo-3.1 (79.1 / 82.6). Evaluated on PAIBench-G, RBench, Physics-IQ, plus the released **Cosmos-HUE** human-eval benchmark.

**Action — forward/inverse dynamics (Table 18):** MT-init (mid-trained) Cosmos3-Super beats specialized baselines on camera-motion FD (0.142° RRE / 0.026 m RTE / 0.99 m ATE vs Lingbot-World 0.299/0.057/2.88 and HY-World 1.5 0.377/0.042/1.39), egocentric FD (PSNR 16.19 vs LOME 9.36), AV inverse dynamics (beats VGGT, DepthAnything3), and DROID robotics FD (PSNR 26.04 vs Ctrl-World 22.99).

**Action — robot policy (Table 19, RoboLab-120):** **Cosmos3-Nano-Policy-DROID** sets a new SOTA — under *specific* instructions **39.7%** avg success vs **π0.5 28.1%**, DreamZero 25.2(/23.9), π0-FAST 14.9, GR00T N1.6 5.3, π0 3.5. Also **#1 on RoboArena** real-world double-blind A/B leaderboard (2026-05-30). Real-robot rollouts shown for "put the screwdriver on the top shelf," multi-object container loading.

**Mid-training transfer (the central action result):**
- **MT-init vs PT-init on LIBERO-10 (new embodiment, Table 20):** at 500 iterations MT-init reaches **24.6%** while PT-init is at **0.0%**; by 2000 iters MT-init hits **97.4%**. Unified action mid-training across embodiments yields a **reusable, transferable action-domain prior** that accelerates downstream adaptation.
- **Synergy study (Figs. 27–28):** co-training across camera / AV / robot / egocentric domains shows broad positive transfer (e.g. AV→camera-FD +0.86 PSNR; Google Robot→WidowX-250 +1.39 FD / +2.29 policy PSNR; egocentric human motion → AgiBot robot adaptation).

**Data scale:** Reasoner ≈ **24.2M samples** (22.0M pre-train sub-selected from Nemotron Nano 2 + curated; 2.2M Physical-AI SFT). Generator pre-trained on large-scale image/video/audio then mid-trained with action / control-transfer / synthetic data.

**Infrastructure:** custom data pipeline, training (selective activation checkpointing, torch.compile, FSDP-class distribution), and serving stacks — **vLLM / TensorRT-LLM** for the reasoner, **vLLM-Omni** for the generator (e.g. Nano 720p T2I latency 1.77 s on B200 with vLLM-Omni vs 4.21 s plain PyTorch).

## Open questions

- **No head-to-head vs JEPA world models.** Cosmos 3 reports SOTA among generative/diffusion systems and VLAs, but is not compared against V-JEPA 2 / LeWorldModel on a shared task — the cross-paradigm gap the wiki keeps flagging remains open.
- **Audio is claimed but under-evaluated** relative to vision/action; the audio generation score (7.31–7.34) sits below Veo-3.1's 7.45.
- **Policy mode is generative (joint video+action diffusion), not latent-space planning.** Does the "predict the visual consequence too" framing buy robustness over a pure action head at deployment, or just at training? Not isolated.
- **Cosmos3-Edge** (4B, on-device) is promised but unreleased — the actual edge-deployment story is still TBD.
- Mid-training transfer is strong within the action modalities NVIDIA chose; the **negative-transfer / interference** cases in the synergy matrices (small Franka subsets) hint the recipe is not free of mixture-design cost.
