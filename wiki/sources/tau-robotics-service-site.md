# Tau Robotics — service site and terms

## Summary

Vendor pages, read on 2026-11-08, for [Tau Robotics](../entities/tau-robotics.md), which sends remotely piloted humanoids to clean homes. It is a paid service, not a product, and the training data is a core part of the business. Its [service terms](https://www.tau-robotics.com/service-terms.html), last updated 24 October 2026, say plainly: "The robot is not autonomous. A person is controlling it."

## Key claims

- **Availability.** Booking is invite-only. The home page says "Now serving Los Angeles and the SF Bay Area." The waitlist also names New York.
- **Standard price (terms).** $50 per humanoid per hour, normally two humanoids, so $100, $200 or $300 for one to three hours.
- **Recurring price (booking page, one hour).** Weekly $60 per visit, every two weeks $80, every four weeks $90.
- **Tasks.** Dusting, wiping surfaces, cleaning toilets, vacuuming and mopping. Staff drop the robots off. **Bathrooms and bedrooms are off-limits by default**, and need opting in at booking.
- **Who controls it.** One trained operator per robot, working on-site at Tau's San Francisco facility, employed on W-2 terms. Not gig workers, and not outside the US.
- **Recording.** "There is no version of this service without recording." Video is continuous, and footage and operator commands train Tau's models. The policy says footage "including unblurred faces" is what the models learn from.
- **Data rights.** A perpetual, worldwide, royalty-free licence to visit footage. Absent a request, footage is kept "for as long as we operate," and customers can request deletion.
- **Children.** An authorized adult must stay and supervise any child who needs it. Tau staff, operators and robots do not supervise children.
- **Damage.** Report within 48 hours. Tau repairs, replaces or pays fair market value at its option, subject to a liability limit.
- **Cancellation.** Free up to 24 hours before. Inside 24 hours, 50% refund.

> [!warning] Contradiction — how autonomous is it?
> The [terms](https://www.tau-robotics.com/service-terms.html) say "The robot is not autonomous. A person is controlling it." The FAQ says robots are "operated by humans and AI," and that a supervisor decides "when to let the robot do tasks autonomously." Co-founder Cornelia Weinzierl told [MIT Technology Review](mit-technology-review-tau-cleaning.md) that the robot can vacuum and mop "without any human help." The terms are the binding document, so this wiki uses them.

> [!warning] Contradiction — can it hear you?
> The FAQ says "you can talk to them and they will answer." The privacy policy says "The robot's microphones are disabled" and that the operator can talk but "cannot hear you."

> [!warning] Contradiction — price
> Press from August quoted $30 an hour ([CBS](cbs-news-tau-robotics.md)). The terms now give $50 per humanoid per hour. MIT Technology Review confirms the change.
