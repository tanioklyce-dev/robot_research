## Summary

Maya Cakmak's personal research overview page. Narrative account of her two research tracks (physically assistive robots and end-user robot programming) with representative projects, motivations, and selected citations. Stated long-term goal: "make personal robot assistants in the home a reality" for persons with motor limitations and older adults. The page is more selective than the [full publications list](hcrlab-publications.md) but provides richer rationale and deployment detail.

## Key claims

- **Research goal**: personal robot assistants in the home for persons with motor limitations and older adults. This is an explicit framing, not just an implied application domain.
- **WHO statistic**: 190 million people globally live with some form of physical or motor limitation.
- **HRI 2020 key finding**: people with severe motor impairments do NOT always prefer more autonomous feeding robots. User autonomy preference is context- and person-specific. [See also: 2025 RO-MAN paper on sense of agency.]
- **Henry Evans deployments (Stretch)** — ongoing since 2021:
  - Summer 2021 (4 weeks, OT Vy Nguyen): self-feeding, face wiping, scratching, lotion, percussion vest, printer, card games, handing rose to wife Jane.
  - Summer 2022: prototype end-user programming tool built for Henry specifically.
  - Summer 2023: expanded abilities; granddaughter connection; student participant.
  - NIH grant secured for continued work.
- **EUP rationale**: Universal robotic capabilities are infeasible. EUP lets general-purpose robots serve individual users' specific needs without a programmer. Eliminates the long-tail customization problem.
- **EUP tools transferred to Hello Robot Stretch SE2** — research results deployed on commercial hardware.
- **FLEX-SDK** (UIST 2022): open-source social robot toolkit from two tablet screens; nine case studies at UW and other institutions over five years.
- **Literature survey**: 45 papers on end-user program specification for robots.
