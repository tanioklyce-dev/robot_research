# CBS News on Tau Robotics

## Summary

Early mainstream coverage of the [Tau Robotics](../entities/tau-robotics.md) service, with on-record statements from its CEO and a critical outside view from [Ken Goldberg](../entities/ken-goldberg.md). Its pricing is out of date; see [Tau's service pages](tau-robotics-service-site.md). Its value is the CEO's stated timeline and the safety concerns.

## Key claims

- **Build cost.** Each robot costs $50,000 to make.
- **Limits.** CEO Alexander Koch: "It doesn't know how to walk upstairs, for example. It doesn't know where to vacuum. That's directed by mostly humans at the moment."
- **Safety stop.** A technician can put the robot into a safety mode that makes it lie down on the floor.
- **Timeline.** Koch's estimates: 1,000 cleanings a week in San Francisco by 2027, other US cities and international by 2028, phasing out technicians around 2028 or 2029.
- **Outside view.** Goldberg says such robots are heavy and can thrash while getting up: "You wouldn't want this thing anywhere near a child or a baby." He estimates another decade before they clean effectively at home, and notes cameras could capture items such as receipts and bills.
- **Pilot size.** CBS says the pilot is "open to 1,000 households." The same article's quoted company target is 1,000 cleanings a week, so the household figure may be a conflation. Tau's site states neither.
