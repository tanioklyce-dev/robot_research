## Summary

[Figure AI](../entities/figure.md) and **[Brookfield](../entities/brookfield.md)** (>$1T AUM, one of the world's largest real-estate owners) announce a three-part partnership: **data collection** for [Helix](../entities/helix.md) inside Brookfield properties, **AI infrastructure** collaboration, and eventual **robot deployment** across Brookfield's portfolio. Brookfield also **invested in Figure's Series C**.

## Key claims

### The asset base being used as a dataset

| | |
|---|---|
| Residential units | **100,000+** |
| Commercial office | **500M sq ft** |
| Logistics space | **160M sq ft** |
| Brookfield AUM | **>$1 trillion** |

*"Using human video capture in a variety of Brookfield environments, Figure will amass critical AI training data for Helix to teach humanoid robots how to move, perceive, and act across a spectrum of human-centric spaces."* Collection had **already launched** at announcement.

### The three strands

1. **Data** — human video capture across residential, office and logistics environments, to build "the world's largest and most diverse real-world humanoid pretraining dataset."
2. **Infrastructure** — the partnership "will explore broader infrastructure collaboration — including support for **next-generation GPU data centers**, real estate for **robotic training environments**, and commercial use cases."
3. **Deployment** — "explore opportunities to deploy humanoid robots within Brookfield's portfolio over time."

### The financial tie

- **"Brookfield has also invested in Figure's Series C fundraising as part of the broader partnership."** Figure's Series C closed at **>$1B on a $39B post-money valuation** (2025-09-16, the day before).
- Brett Adcock: Brookfield's scale gives Figure *"an unmatched platform to capture massive amounts of real-world, humanlike navigation and manipulation data across a variety of household environments."*
- Stewart Upson (Brookfield, Head of Global Strategy): *"unlocking direct deployment opportunities over time with a view to enhancing our operations."*

## Assessment

> [!note] Property portfolios as training corpora — a genuinely new asset class
> This is the first instance in the wiki of **real estate being monetised as robot training data**. Brookfield's holdings are valuable to Figure not as places to sell robots but as **environment diversity** — 100,000 residential units is a distribution over kitchens, hallways and clutter that no lab can stage. The wiki's other data strategies buy *labour* ([Index](../entities/figure-index.md) pays creators, teleoperation pays operators); this one buys **access to space**. Worth watching as a template: whoever owns a lot of varied human-occupied interior square footage owns a robotics data moat, and most of them do not know it yet.

> [!warning] The data supplier is also an investor, and also a prospective customer
> Brookfield supplies the data, invested in the round, and is named as a future deployment site. Each strand is unremarkable alone; together they mean **no strand is priced independently**. Nothing here is improper, but it does mean "partnership" numbers should not be read as market validation.

> [!warning] Occupancy and consent go unaddressed in both posts
> Neither this post nor [Go-Big](figure-project-go-big.md) says whether the residential units are **occupied or vacant**, who is filmed, or on what consent terms. [Go-Big](figure-project-go-big.md) says video is *"collected passively as people do behaviors in real Brookfield homes"* — "people" and "passively" are the only description of the human subjects anywhere. The landlord's consent is not the tenant's.
