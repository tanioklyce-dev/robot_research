## The numbers

- **$150 million** Series B at a **$1.25 billion** valuation, announced **2026-02-05**.
- **Led by B Capital.**
- Participating: **DFJ Growth, Salesforce Ventures, Eric Schmidt**, plus existing investors Juniper Ventures, Menlo Ventures, Lightspeed Venture Partners, South Park Commons, Wing Venture Capital.
- **Headcount not disclosed.**

Named customers and partners in the announcement: **Arc Institute, Mayo Clinic, Microsoft, Prima Mente, Rakuten.**

## What the money is for

Two stated directions, plus continued foundational research:

1. **Intentional design of models** — expanding the "model design environment" ([Silico](goodfire-silico-robotics-vision.md)) so that models are built deliberately rather than only scaled.
2. **Scientific discovery via model-to-human knowledge transfer** — extracting from models what they have learned and humans have not.

Capabilities highlighted: *"decompose model internals,"* hallucination reduction, and biomarker identification. Silico's three verticals — **life sciences, robotics and vision, and language models** — are named here as well as on the product page.

## The claim worth recording

> "[Interpretability is] the toolset for a new domain of science: a way to form hypotheses, run experiments, and ultimately design intelligence."

And, on what it buys operationally, that interpretability lets teams *"understand and edit models."*

> [!note] Why a funding post is worth a source page in this wiki
> Not for the valuation. For the **positioning**, which is the thing a $150M round at $1.25B actually evidences: a market exists that will pay for interpretability **as tooling**, sold to model builders outside frontier labs, with **robotics named as one of three verticals**.
>
> The wiki has tracked interpretability as an [Anthropic](../entities/anthropic.md)-internal safety programme ([mechanistic interpretability](../concepts/safety/mechanistic-interpretability.md), 2 sources, both pedagogical). This is the first evidence here that it has become an **industry** — and the first suggestion that robot-policy internals are a commercial target rather than only an academic one. Whether the tooling works is a separate question this source cannot answer; see the [product page](goodfire-silico-robotics-vision.md) for how little is substantiated.
