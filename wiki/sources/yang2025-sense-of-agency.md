## Summary

Two-part survey study investigating how robot autonomy levels affect users' sense of agency and robot preferences across household tasks with varying risk. Tests four robot autonomy levels using Hello Robot Stretch 3 illustrations. Key finding: sense of agency is primarily determined by whether a third party controls the robot's programming, not just the level of autonomous execution. End-user programmed robots preserve sense of agency even when acting autonomously, making EUP a compelling design target.

## Key claims

- **Four robot autonomy levels tested**:
  - (A) Fully autonomous — robot decides and acts independently
  - (B) End-user programmed — robot acts per user's own program
  - (C) Third-party teleoperated — remote professional controls the robot
  - (D) Fully user-controlled — user has direct control at all times
- **Two factors driving sense of agency**: (1) whether the robot acts autonomously (reduces agency); (2) whether a third party is involved in programming or operation (reduces agency further).
- **EUP preserves agency**: An end-user programmed robot (B) highly preserves sense of agency even when it acts autonomously — because the user is the author of the robot's behavior. This is the study's headline finding.
- **Risk sensitivity**: In high-risk scenarios (e.g., preparing a snack for a child with allergies, medical tasks), participants strongly prefer robots that prioritize user control. In low-risk contexts, higher autonomy is acceptable.
- **Trust mediates third-party preference**: Trust in the third-party operator also shapes autonomy preferences — users who trust the operator are more comfortable with third-party teleoperation (C).
- **Model**: Sense of agency can be preserved "if the robot is programmed by the user, instead of relying on a third party to control the robot."
- **Platform**: Hello Robot Stretch 3 used for all four robot option illustrations in the survey.
- **Funding**: NIH NIBIB grant #1R01EB034580-01 (NRI: Adaptive Teleoperation Interfaces for In-Home Assistive Robots); NSF #2313998.
