## Summary

Cross-institutional reflection on methodology for including people with motor impairments in PAR research design, development, and evaluation. Covers three PAR projects (robot-assisted feeding, assistive teleoperation, shared control) conducted independently across three labs (UW, TU Dortmund, University of Michigan). Discusses three dimensions — individual vs. community-level insights, logistic burden on users vs. researchers, benefit to researchers vs. community — and offers shared recommendations. Workshop paper (5 pages).

## Key claims

- **Problem**: Nearly half of PAR user studies do not involve participants with target disabilities — driven by recruitment challenges, small sample sizes, transportation logistics, and systemic access barriers faced by PwD.
- **Three projects covered**: (a) Assistive feeding with Kinova JACO Gen2 robot arm; (b) Assistive teleoperation with a mobile manipulator; (c) Shared control with a robot arm.
- **Three analytical dimensions**:
  1. Individual vs. Community-Level Insights: deep research with few participants vs. broad research with many.
  2. Logistic burden: how to navigate differential burden on end-users vs. researchers.
  3. Benefit: what do researchers vs. community members get from the collaboration?
- **Methods employed**: Community research (CBPR), remote studies, home deployments, in-the-wild study at a trade fair — each offering different tradeoffs.
- **Participatory design**: Collaborative approach with end-users throughout design, not just evaluation. All three projects use this.
- **Empowerment design**: "Nothing about us without us" — empowering users with disabilities to contribute to design and build the technologies themselves.
- **Finding from all three projects**: Participants valued their inclusion in the design process of a device developed specifically for them.
- **Cross-institutional insight**: Despite independent origins, methodological insights generalized across all three projects.
