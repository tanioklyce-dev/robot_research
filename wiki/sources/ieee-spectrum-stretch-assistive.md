## Summary
Feature article in IEEE Spectrum (November 2023 issue) documenting Henry Evans — a quadriplegic user — and his daily life with [Hello Robot Stretch](../entities/stretch.md) as an assistive device. Provides the most concrete primary narrative of Stretch in real-world assistive use, naming key people at Hello Robot and the research background (Robots for Humanity, Charlie Kemp's Georgia Tech lab).

## Key claims

- Stretch costs **$20,000** — a fraction of PR2's $400,000; movable by one person.
- ~2 kg lift capacity; limited DOF (vertical, extend/retract, wrist); inexpensive Amazon-sourced gripper.
- **Case study (Henry Evans, quadriplegic):** scratches itches, moves blankets, retrieves items, transports laundry, plays cards with granddaughter, assists with meals, delivered flowers independently.
- Net impact: restored agency, reduced caregiver burden (wife Jane Evans), restored Henry's sense of purpose and dignity.
- **"Assistive autonomy"** concept: user-directed operations via customizable GUI + camera views; not fully autonomous but user-controlled.
- Limitations: cannot manipulate user's body, no stair navigation, requires significant technical setup.
