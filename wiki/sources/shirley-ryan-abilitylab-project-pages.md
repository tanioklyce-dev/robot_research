# Shirley Ryan AbilityLab project pages

## Summary

Five project pages from the [Shirley Ryan AbilityLab](../entities/shirley-ryan-abilitylab.md). Three describe engineering work relevant to this wiki: the **COMET** center on machines that work alongside therapists, a **lightweight powered leg** for older users, and a **manual wheelchair that moves while the user stands**. Two are clinical and are recorded briefly. These are institutional descriptions of aims and designs, dated 2017–2020. They report no results.

## Key claims

**COMET — Collaborative Machines Enhancing Therapies** (Robotics Lab, page dated 2018)
- A federally funded rehabilitation engineering research center that follows three funding cycles of an earlier center on robot-assisted therapy for stroke and spinal cord injury.
- **The lesson it draws from that earlier work:** therapy robots that physically guide a patient through repeated motion, with the therapist as a bystander, gave more practice but variable gains and slow clinical acceptance. The earlier center *"revealed limits"* and led to passive mechanical devices, sensors, movement augmentation, body-machine interfaces and challenge-based strategies.
- COMET's stated strategy is machines that involve both therapist and patient. Its projects: passive wearable arm devices, a wearable sensor that encourages movement practice, a soft exoskeleton for gait after stroke, a robotic treadmill for propulsion training, combined brain and muscle feedback for the hand, a wearable balance trainer (gyroscopic actuators appear in its publication list), and a body-machine interface for recovering muscle control.
- It includes an observational study of what predicts whether clinicians take up such devices.

**Evaluation of a Lightweight Powered Leg** (Center for Bionic Medicine, page dated 2020)
- The page states that older people are over 90% of those with a lower-limb amputation in the United States, and that existing powered legs are designed for younger users and are too heavy for them.
- **Hybrid knee:** an *active variable transmission* that changes motor power and speed with the activity. The knee gives no power in level walking and full power for standing up or climbing stairs, so the motor can be smaller, lighter and quieter.
- **Adaptive ankle:** a non-backdrivable cam-based transmission that positions the ankle only when the foot is off the ground, again allowing a smaller motor and battery.
- Planned testing with people with above-knee amputations against a commercial passive leg, and use of muscle signals from the residual limb to position the leg when not walking.

**Manual Standing Wheelchair** (Center for Bionic Medicine, marked complete)
- The page states that about 1.7 million Americans use wheelchairs or scooters and that 90% use manual chairs.
- The design lets the user propel the chair seated, standing or anywhere between, with a hand-drive mechanism meant to reduce shoulder strain, restraints, and anti-tip wheels that deploy when standing. A US patent on the hand drive issued in 2019.
- Stated benefits of standing: reach to counters and shelves, physiological benefits, and eye-level interaction.

**Two clinical pages**
- **Intraoperative Hand Measurements** (Muscle Physiology Lab, 2017): biomechanical measurement during tendon-transfer surgery to restore hand function. No robotics content.
- **Neuromodulation Research Study** (2019): a recruitment notice for non-invasive stimulation paired with walking and reach-and-grasp training after spinal cord injury. No robotics content.

## Analysis

- **COMET's history is a caution about a whole category.** A hospital that ran a robot-therapy center for three funding cycles concluded that robots guiding repetitive motion were not enough, and moved toward simpler and more collaborative devices. That is first-hand institutional evidence that more automation did not translate into clinical uptake.
- **The powered leg takes the opposite actuator route from the [open-source bionic leg](open-source-bionic-leg-paper.md).** The open-source leg uses a low fixed ratio and a large motor for bandwidth and transparency. The lightweight leg uses a variable transmission and a non-backdrivable ankle to shrink the motor, accepting that it is powered only some of the time. The two come from the same center and target different users.
- **Evidence weight.** Project descriptions only. The papers each page lists are the place to look for results; none is ingested.
