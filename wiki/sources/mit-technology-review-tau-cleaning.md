# MIT Technology Review on a Tau Robotics cleaning

## Summary

A first-person account of two booked [Tau Robotics](../entities/tau-robotics.md) cleanings, by a reporter who did not initially identify as a journalist. It is the most useful independent evidence in this wiki on what a teleoperated home humanoid does in practice. The robot was slow and error-prone but did real cleaning, with a remote human in control.

## Key claims

- **Price change.** $30 for one hour with one robot when the reporter booked; now $60 per weekly cleaning with two robots, or $100 one-time.
- **Control.** Piloted remotely through cameras in the head and wrists. Bed-making and laundry folding need "kinematic guidance," where the pilot performs the motions.
- **Hardware.** About 4.5 feet tall, with a 3D-printed head and 3D-printed claw hands.
- **First visit.** It staggered on eight entry stairs and was carried up the last few interior steps. It stalled repeatedly and left early when heat shut down its operator's equipment. Tau comped the visit.
- **Second visit.** Cleaned the kitchen except the stove and cleaned a toilet, reattaching a wand head three times. It left taps dripping, knocked over a closed bottle of olive oil, and toppled an air purifier it could not right.
- **Value.** "Thorough but slow." The reporter judged it not a great bargain even at $30 an hour.
- **Scale.** "Only a couple hundred cleanings so far."
- **Autonomy claim.** Co-founder Cornelia Weinzierl says the robot can already vacuum and mop without human help. See the contradiction on [Tau's service pages](tau-robotics-service-site.md).
