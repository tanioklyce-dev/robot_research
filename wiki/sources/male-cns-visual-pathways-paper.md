# The organization of visual pathways in the Drosophila brain (Hoeller, Zhao, Nern, Rogers, Romani & Reiser; Cell 2026)

## Summary

**Tracing vision through an entire brain.** Using the male connectome, the authors follow signals from photoreceptors through the optic lobes — *"layered, retinotopic regions containing two-thirds of the brain's neurons"* — into the central brain, with a network-based information-flow analysis. They report *"a multilayered architecture of pathway classes with distinct input mixtures"*: visual-input channels *"spread broadly yet converge in focal regions apparently specialized for particular features and fine spatial sampling."* Propagating signals through the pathways predicts receptive-field structure and feature selectivity that match physiology and *"extend to thousands of uncharacterized neuron types"* — a connectome-derived functional annotation at scale. For the wiki's [connectome](../concepts/bio/connectome.md) page this is the *structural-prior* use of a connectome (predicting function from wiring) applied to the whole visual system of one animal; the Reiser lab's earlier [flyvis](../entities/flyvis.md)-style connectome-constrained work is the ancestor.
