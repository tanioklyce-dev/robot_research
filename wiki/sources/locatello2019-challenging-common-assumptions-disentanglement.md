## Summary

**Locatello, Bauer, Lucic, Rätsch, Gelly, Schölkopf & Bachem** (ETH Zurich / MPI-IS / Google Brain; **ICML 2019 Best Paper**). Two contributions, and both are negative results delivered with unusual rigour:

1. **A theorem**: unsupervised learning of disentangled representations is **fundamentally impossible without inductive biases on *both* the model and the data.**
2. **The largest reproduction study in the field at the time**: **over 12,000 models**, six methods, six metrics, seven datasets — finding that the methods do enforce what their losses encourage, and that **none of the downstream claims survive.**

**Why it matters to this wiki.** It is the sharpest available statement of the [inductive bias](../concepts/learning/inductive-bias.md) page's central claim — not "bias helps," but **"without bias the task is not merely hard, it is ill-posed."** And it is the reason the wiki's [β-VAE](beta-vae-paper.md) page carries a warning: β-VAE's disentanglement claims are exactly what this paper failed to reproduce as *useful*.

## Abstract (verbatim)

> "The key idea behind the unsupervised learning of disentangled representations is that real-world data is generated by a few explanatory factors of variation which can be recovered by unsupervised learning algorithms. In this paper, we provide a sober look at recent progress in the field and challenge some common assumptions. We first theoretically show that the unsupervised learning of disentangled representations is fundamentally impossible without inductive biases on both the models and the data. Then, we train more than 12000 models covering most prominent methods and evaluation metrics in a reproducible large-scale experimental study on seven different data sets. We observe that while the different methods successfully enforce properties 'encouraged' by the corresponding losses, well-disentangled models seemingly cannot be identified without supervision. Furthermore, increased disentanglement does not seem to lead to a decreased sample complexity of learning for downstream tasks."

## The impossibility theorem (§3)

> **Theorem 1.** For `d > 1`, let `z ∼ P` denote any distribution which admits a density `p(z) = Π_{i=1..d} p(z_i)`. Then there exists an **infinite family of bijective functions** `f : supp(z) → supp(z)` such that `∂f_i(u)/∂u_j ≠ 0` almost everywhere for all `i` and `j` (i.e. **`z` and `f(z)` are completely entangled**) and `P(z ≤ u) = P(f(z) ≤ u)` for all `u` (i.e. **they have the same marginal distribution**).

The argument in plain terms: take the "intuitive" notion of disentanglement — a change in one ground-truth factor should change one dimension of the representation. Given any factorized prior `p(z)` and any `P(x|z)`, there is an infinite family of **completely entangled** latent spaces that are **statistically indistinguishable** from the disentangled one. An unsupervised method sees only the data distribution. It therefore has **no information** that could separate the good representation from the infinitely many bad ones.

> [!note] Why this is the strongest version of the inductive-bias argument in the wiki
> [ViT](vit-paper.md) and [TDV](tdv-paper.md) show that the *optimal strength* of an inductive bias falls as data grows. This paper shows a case where **no amount of data helps at all**, because the ambiguity is not a sampling problem but an identifiability one. More data gives you a better estimate of the same distribution, and the distribution does not determine the answer.
>
> The authors are careful about what follows: *"this does not necessarily mean it is an impossible endeavour in practice. After all, real world generative models may have a certain structure that could be exploited through suitably chosen inductive biases. However, Theorem 1 clearly shows that inductive biases are required **both for the models** (so that we find a specific set of solutions) **and for the data sets** (such that these solutions match the true generative model)."*
>
> **The bias is not an aid to the objective. It is the only thing that makes the objective well-posed at all.** Recorded on [inductive bias](../concepts/learning/inductive-bias.md).

## The reproduction study (§4–5)

Six methods (all VAE-plus-a-regularizer: β-VAE, FactorVAE, β-TCVAE, DIP-VAE-I/II, AnnealedVAE), six disentanglement metrics, seven datasets (dSprites, Cars3D, and variants including one textured with Munch's *The Scream*), **>12,000 models**. They also released **`disentanglement_lib`** and **>10,000 trained models** as baselines — an unusually serious reproducibility commitment for 2019.

### The three findings

**(i) The methods do what their losses say.** Dimensions of the representation (taken to be the mean) become uncorrelated as the regularizer demands. The losses work.

**(ii) That does not produce reliably disentangled representations, and you cannot tell which runs did.** *"We do not find any evidence that the considered models can be used to reliably learn disentangled representations in an unsupervised manner as **random seeds and hyperparameters seem to matter more than the model choice**. Furthermore, good trained models seemingly cannot be identified without access to ground-truth labels even if we are allowed to transfer good hyperparameter values across data sets."*

The vivid version, from §5.3: **"a good run with a bad hyperparameter can beat a bad run with a good hyperparameter."** Seed variance swamps method choice.

**(iii) Disentanglement does not buy the downstream benefit it was sold on.** *"For the considered models and data sets, we cannot validate the assumption that disentanglement is useful for downstream tasks, for example through a decreased sample complexity of learning."*

> [!warning] Finding (ii) is a methodology result that outlives the topic
> "Random seeds matter more than model choice" and "you cannot identify the good runs without labels" are not facts about disentanglement — they are facts about **how a subfield can accumulate published improvements that are seed variance**, when model selection quietly uses the test signal.
>
> This wiki has a live instance of the same shape: the [VLA success-rate audit](../syntheses/platforms/vla-success-rate-audit.md) finds **ten models within 1.2 percentage points** on LIBERO. Nothing in that literature reports seed variance, and the [LIBERO protocol](../entities/libero.md) is 50 episodes per task. **A 1.2-point spread across ten models with unreported seed variance is exactly the situation Locatello et al. found in 2019** — and their diagnosis, that model selection was implicitly consuming supervision nobody accounted for, transfers directly.

## Open questions / TBD

- **Nobody has run the seed-variance study for VLAs.** Locatello et al.'s finding (ii) is directly reproducible on [LIBERO](../entities/libero.md): train one architecture at `N` seeds, report the spread, compare it to the 1.2-point cross-model tier. Cheap, and it would either validate or dissolve a whole leaderboard. Related: [the proposed latent-inspection experiment](../syntheses/projects/latent-inspection-policy-collapse.md).
- **What the robotics equivalent of the impossibility result is.** If disentanglement is unidentifiable without bias on model *and* data, the analogous question for a robot policy — which task structure is identifiable from demonstrations alone — has, as far as this wiki knows, no answer.
- **The follow-up literature** (weakly-supervised and few-label disentanglement, largely by the same authors) is un-ingested.
