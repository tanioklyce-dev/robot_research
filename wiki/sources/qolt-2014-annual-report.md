## Summary

The Year-8 annual report of the [Quality of Life Technology ERC](../entities/cmu-qolt-center.md) — a working snapshot of the center in its 8th of 10 planned years (Year 8 ended 2014-05-31). The report **settles the wiki's wind-down question**: it contains milestone tables "Planned for Year 9" and "Planned for Year 10," a "Business Plan for Post-Graduation Self-Sufficiency" (§5.3.3), and an REU site renewal "for 2014–2016" — i.e., the center was operating on the standard 10-year ERC lifecycle with **graduation at the end of Year 10 (May 31, 2016)**, not terminating at the 2014 date shown on the base NSF award record. It also documents a leadership transition (Kanade → Siewiorek), a mid-life research reorganization (the Mobility & Manipulation thrust wound down into a "QoLTbots" testbed), and the center's explicitly *symbiosis-over-autonomy* design philosophy.

## Key claims

**Lifecycle / wind-down (the settling facts):**

- The report distinguishes "milestones already achieved through 2013 and those expected to be achieved by the end of Year 8 (5/31/14)," adding "Some milestones originally targeted for Year 8 will be achieved in Year 9" (strategy section, ~p. 29).
- Milestone & deliverable tables run four columns: "Achieved through Year 7 / Planned for Year 8 / Planned for Year 9 / Planned for Year 10" (Table QoLT-Strategy-4, pp. 36–37).
- §5.3.3 "Business Plan for Post-Graduation Self-Sufficiency" (p. 144) plans core funding beyond "NSF's investment" via provost overhead return, state support, etc., with assured retention of office/lab space "upon graduation."
- The QoLT REU site was renewed "for 2014–2016" (§3.4).
- 2013 Site Visit Team criticism, quoted with the center's rebuttal: "The sustainability plan is overly reliant on NIH funding post-graduation" (p. v). Another SWOT item: no in-center expertise on CMS/HIPAA policy.

**Leadership and organization:**

- **Dan Siewiorek is Center Director**, "unanimously selected by a Search Committee constituted to conduct a nationwide search for a permanent replacement for Founding Director, Takeo Kanade" (§5.1.2). Rory Cooper remains Co-Director and PI of the Pitt sub-award. (This explains why the NSF/Grantome record lists Siewiorek as PI.)
- Four research thrusts as of Year 8: **Perception & Awareness** (pioneered the first-person-vision camera perspective), **Mobility & Manipulation** ("wind down in Year 8; most projects moved to QoLTbots" — faculty: Atkeson (lead), Brewer, Geyer, **Sidd Srinivasa**), **Human-System Interaction** (Forlizzi lead; Bigham, Collinger, Cooper, Dey…), **Person & Society**.

**Design philosophy (wiki-relevant):**

> "Whereas the goal of traditional robot autonomy is intelligence to function with minimal human involvement, the goal of QoLT **symbiosis** is for intelligent systems to function in concert with a person." (Project Summary, p. i)

An explicit 2014 institutional rejection of maximal autonomy as the design target — anticipating by ~6 years the empirical autonomy-preference findings in [levels of autonomy in assistive robotics](../syntheses/assistive/levels-of-autonomy-in-assistive-robotics.md).

**Systems and testbeds (Year-8 status):**

- **QoLTbots** testbed: HERB, PerMMA-1, PerMMA-2, **Strong Arm** (prototype patient-transfer robot; goal: safely transfer users up to 250 lbs on a 6° slope), **MEBot** (curb-climbing wheelchair base: 8" curbs in 20 s vs PerMMA-2's 3" in 150 s); OpenRAVE motion-planning architecture founded here; assistive teleoperation + handover algorithms on HERB.
- **Health & wellness**: Health Kiosk (3 prototype generations, field study with 40+ older adults), dWellSense (~12-household, ≥8-month deployment), Cueing Kitchen.
- **Safe driving**: Virtual Valet prototype, DriveCap Advisor (smartphone ML flagging aggressive driving, 80–90% accurate in a preliminary field study), drive-by-wire integration with a driving simulator (30-participant evaluation).
- **Virtual coaches**: InForm Exercise Coach (osteoarthritis PT), Stroke Therapy Coach; emotion recognition from voice/face at ≥ human-observer accuracy, integrated into the coaches. A power-wheelchair repositioning coach study showed reduced posture-adjustment lapses vs control.
- **Commercialization**: the **NAViSection** project won an NSF I-Corps Teams grant ($50k, 6 months) and formed the university spin-off **Navity** (entrepreneurial lead Nahom Beyene) — the one named QoLT Foundry-pipeline company in the report.
- Market context the center cites: Medicare's 30-day readmission penalty "has opened providers' eyes to the value of home healthcare technology like QoLT" (p. ii).
