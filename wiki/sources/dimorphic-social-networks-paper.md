# Networks of sexually dimorphic neurons that regulate social behaviors in Drosophila (Rubin et al.; Current Biology 2026)

> [!warning] Abstract-only ingest

## Summary

**From connectome to genetic tools to behaviour.** The male-specific pC1/pC1x neurons — long described as the sensory-integration site for mating and aggression — sit *"in an expansive and highly intertwined male-specific network."* The **48** connectomically defined pC1/pC1x cell types have heterogeneous connectivity, a subset with strong input from identified sensory pathways; the team built **genetic driver lines** for them and used those to assign distinct roles in *"social acoustic signaling and male-male interactions,"* and describes motifs by which pC1 and dimorphic aIPg neurons co-regulate behaviour. It is the companion that closes the loop the [male CNS paper](male-cns-connectome-paper.md) opens: connectome → cell types → reagents → behavioural test. The preprint used the older P1/pC1x nomenclature and named mAL and aSP-a as network partners.
