# Household robots compared — for research and for the home today

A comparison of the home-capable robots on sale or announced as of **2026-11-08**, checked against every robot page in this wiki plus web searches, and judged two separate ways: **as a research platform**, and **as something useful in an ordinary consumer's home today**. The two rankings barely overlap. A robot that is open and instrumentable is rarely one that does your chores, and the products that sell a chore are mostly closed. For platform lists beyond the home, see the [platforms comparison](robot-platforms-comparison.md) and [humanoid survey](humanoid-platforms-survey.md).

> [!note] Bottom line (2026-11-08)
> **For research:** [Stretch 4](../../entities/stretch.md) is the best shipping platform, but plan to port software and re-validate results. [NORI A3](../../entities/nori-a3.md) ($1,688) is the cheap bimanual experiment.
>
> **For a consumer today:** buy a good robot vacuum. It is the only household robot that reliably does a chore unattended. If you live in California and hate folding, [Isaac 0](../../entities/weave-robotics.md) is a real but slow, remotely assisted product. **No general-purpose home robot is yet useful to an ordinary household.** Pre-order only with money you are prepared to lose to delays.

## How the two lenses work

**Research value** asks whether a lab can get the robot, program it, and measure it: availability, developer access, sensing and payload, and published in-home evidence.

**Consumer usefulness today** asks what a non-expert gets this week: can it be delivered, does it finish a chore without you, how much supervision and remote human access it needs, and what happens when it fails.

"Evidence" means a published result with stated trials or independent reviews. A vendor video is not evidence.

## Lens 1: as a research platform

The test task list is the one this page began with: navigate a home, pick things up off the floor, put away dishes, open cans. ✅ is published evidence or designed capability, ⚠️ is plausible but unproven, and ❌ is unsupported.

| Task | Stretch 4 | NORI A3 | G1 EDU | Isaac 1 | NEO |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Navigate a house** | ✅ Holonomic base, 3D LiDAR, Nav2 | ⚠️ 2D LiDAR; base type unpublished | ⚠️ Falls remain the risk in clutter | ⚠️ Vendor claim | ⚠️ Vendor claim |
| **Floor pickup** | ⚠️ Earlier Stretch evidence; floor reach unstated, needs re-validation | ⚠️ Lift column; floor reach unpublished | ❌ Requires whole-body balance; no measured result | ⚠️ Vendor claim for clothes and toys | ⚠️ Vendor claim |
| **Put away dishes** | ⚠️ Payload now adequate; one arm; no published result | ⚠️ 1.5 kg per arm; no depth; breakage risk | ❌ 5-DOF arms, ~3 kg | ❌ Not a listed feature | ⚠️ Advertised; delivered autonomy "basic" |
| **Open cans** | ❌ One arm cannot hold and open | ❌ No demonstration | ❌ No demonstration | ❌ Not offered | ❌ Not offered |

**The evidence.**

- **Stretch 2/3.** [RUM](../../sources/robot-utility-models-paper.md) reached **90% average success** across five tasks in real homes (74.4% raw plus 15.6% after retries). Only its tissue and paper-bag pickups relate to this list. [OK-Robot](../../sources/ok-robot-project-page.md) reached 58.5% on open-vocabulary pick-and-drop in 10 homes, and 82% in uncluttered ones. Long-term use is documented in the [in-home deployments synthesis](../assistive/long-term-in-home-robot-deployments.md).
- **Stretch 4.** None yet in this wiki. Its base, wrist, sensors and software all changed, and [Stretch 3 is sold out](https://hello-stretch3.com/purchase). The [`stretch_ai` README](../../sources/stretch-ai-github-readme.md) names only Stretch 3, so **treat those results as grounds for optimism, not a specification**.
- **G1.** [HIW-500](../../entities/hiw-500.md) (500+ hours of teleoperated work in 12 homes) and [UnifoLM-WLA-1.0](../../sources/unifolm-wla-1-project-page.md) give open data and weights. WLA reports video carousels and no numbers.
- **Memo.** The site shows dishwasher loading, labelled autonomous, without success rates or trial counts. No source reviewed here shows any robot opening a can.

**Why Stretch 4 leads.** It can be received now. It has a passively stable base, a telescoping arm on a 120 cm lift, 2.5–4 kg payload, 3D LiDAR and wrist depth. Its ROS 2 and Python stack is open, with new [documentation](https://docs.hello-robot.com/) covering puppet-teleop data collection and VLM grasping. Its lineage has the only rigorous in-home data. Confirm the lowest gripper height with Hello Robot, because the [datasheet](../../sources/hello-robot-stretch-4-datasheet.md) as captured here does not state it.

**Budget.** $29,950 + $1,495 docking station = **$31,445**, or **$34,440** with the $2,995 Jetson Orin NX ([purchase page](https://hello-robot.com/purchase/)).

**First experiment.** Port one RUM policy and rerun its tissue and bag pickups at the published 10 trials per environment. That shows how much of the old evidence transfers.

| If your research needs… | Choose | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A home-capable platform now | **Stretch 4** | As above |
| Two arms at lab budget | **TIAGo Pro** or **[Reachy 2](../../entities/reachy.md)** | Two 7-DoF arms with about 3 kg per arm; get quotes |
| Bimanual experiments under $5k | **NORI A3** | Lowest-priced redundant arms; depth and durability unknown |
| A fully open build | **[XLeRobot](../../entities/xlerobot.md)** | Inspectable and cheap, with low payload and 5-DOF arms |
| Legged robot safe around people | **[Fauna Sprout](../../entities/fauna-robotics.md)** | Soft, 22.7 kg, full SDK; about $50,000 by press reports |
| Whole-body humanoid work | **G1 EDU**, or G1-D for data | Avoid the $13,500 model: Unitree's table shows no secondary development |

## Lens 2: useful in a consumer's home today

| Chore | What you can actually get today | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Vacuuming and mopping** | Robot vacuums | ✅ **Solved enough to buy.** Mature, unattended, and the baseline any new robot must beat. |
| **Folding laundry** | [Isaac 0](../../sources/eweek-weave-isaac-0.md), California only | ⚠️ **Real, narrow.** 30–90 minutes per load (eWeek), with remote specialists correcting mistakes. |
| **Picking up small floor clutter** | [Saros Z70](../../sources/roborock-saros-z70.md) arm | ❌ **Not reliably.** Limited to items under 300 g. TechRadar "couldn't get it to automatically tidy away anything at all." |
| **Tidying rooms, making beds** | Isaac 1, on pre-order | ❌ **Not delivered.** No customer shipment reported. |
| **Dishes** | Nothing purchasable | ❌ Memo shows it, and is in beta at best. |
| **Fetching and carrying for someone with a disability** | [Stretch](../../entities/stretch.md), mostly teleoperated | ⚠️ **Genuinely useful for a few.** See below. |
| **Companionship and play** | [Microduck](../../entities/microduck.md), [Reachy Mini](../../entities/reachy-mini.md), Go2 | ⚠️ **Fun, not useful in the chore sense.** |
| **Cooking, opening cans** | Nothing | ❌ |

**What to do as a consumer.**

1. **Buy a good robot vacuum.** Check current reviews for vacuuming performance. Don't pay extra for an arm unless remote pickup or a mobility need justifies it.
2. **Consider Isaac 0 only if** you are in California, folding is a real burden, and you accept that people may correct folds through its cameras.
3. **Pre-order NEO, Isaac 1 or Flourish 1 only as an early adopter.** Deposits are refundable, but delivery dates, autonomy and failure handling are unproven.
4. **Don't buy a G1, R1, Sprout or research platform for chores.** None comes with household chore software.

**Questions to ask before paying for any home robot.**

- **Is it secure?** [UniPwn](https://www.ieee-ras.org/news/exploit-allows-for-takeover-of-fleets-of-unitree-robots/) and [CVE-2026-27509](https://www.sentinelone.com/vulnerability-database/cve-2026-27509) allow root takeover of Go2 robots, and the suggested mitigation is an isolated network. Patch status was not confirmed.
- **Who is watching?** NEO uses scheduled remote experts, and both Weave products use teleoperation. Camera access by staff is part of the product.
- **What fails, and how often?** Only Flourish states a success rate (80%), without a protocol. Its Terms admit it "will sometimes fail at tasks it performed correctly the day before."
- **What stops working offline or after the subscription?** Flourish depends on cloud compute for its learned behaviour.
- **What about warranty and safety?** Flourish is sold with no warranty or returns in the US, and no vendor here cites a home-robot safety certification.

**The real exception: assistive use.** For a person with a severe disability, a mostly teleoperated robot can already change daily life. Keith Platt, who is quadriplegic, went from nearly two hours to a few minutes for a protein shake on Stretch ([TechCrunch](../../sources/techcrunch-hello-robot-homes-2026.md)). Autonomy matters less there because the alternative is another person's help. Stretch 4 is still certified only for lab and research use, so this path needs Hello Robot's involvement. TechRadar also flags the Saros Z70's remote arm as having potential for users with mobility issues. [Kinova Jaco](../../entities/kinova-jaco.md), a wheelchair-mounted arm sold as a medical device, is the most established product here: a Jaco-based system let a person with quadriplegia feed himself at home across [10 meals over five days](../../sources/nanavati2025-feeding-out-of-lab.md).

## When these answers should change

- **Research:** a measured Stretch 4 household result, an official `stretch_ai` port, or a G1 policy result with trial counts in homes.
- **Consumer:** independent owner reports on Isaac 1, NEO or NORI A3 that include intervention rates, or Memo going on sale with a published dishwasher success rate.

> [!note] Prices drift
> Between May and November, Stretch's Jetson add-on rose from $2,495 to $2,995, Flourish's deposit fell from $1,555 to $200, and Isaac 0 went from a reported $7,999 to $3,999. Re-check vendor pages before purchase.
