# Maya Cakmak — Research

Paul G. Allen School of Computer Science & Engineering, University of Washington.
Lab: Human-Centered Robotics Lab (http://hcrlab.cs.washington.edu/)

## Research goal

"The long term goal of my research is to make personal robot assistants in the home a reality. Such robots can bring independence to persons with motor limitations, enable older adults to age in place, and improve the quality of people's lives."

## Robots for Access

Research centers on including persons with disabilities (PwDs) throughout all work; this inclusion is recognized as best practice for inclusive design. Multi-year focus across physically assistive robots and end-user robot programming. Awards: Best paper ICRA 2021, Best paper HRI 2023.

## Physically Assistive Robots

WHO: 190 million people globally live with some form of physical or motor limitation.

**Key project timeline:**
- **ASSETS 2019** — Contextual inquiry: meals with four individuals needing feeding assistance + caregivers
- **Bangladesh visits** — Explored low-cost assistive robots at assisted-living facilities and community centers
- **HRI 2020** — Key finding: people with severe motor impairments do NOT always prefer more autonomous feeding robots
- **IROS 2021** — Unified formalism representing teleoperation interfaces as finite state machines

**Henry Evans long-term deployments** (quadriplegic user, stroke; ongoing since 2021 using Stretch):
- Summer 2021: 4-week visit by OT Vy Nguyen. Tasks achieved: feeding himself, wiping face, scratching itches, applying lotion, operating percussion vest, using printer, playing card games, handing a rose to wife Jane.
- Summer 2022: Prototype end-user programming tool developed specifically for Henry
- Summer 2023: Expanded abilities; student participant; connection with granddaughter
- Funding: NIH grant secured

**Human-robot handovers:**
- A decade of work on handovers
- ICRA 2021 Best HRI Paper Award
- Recent paper: method selects handover poses accounting for human motor limitations (affordance-aware)

**Feeding robots** (with UW Personal Robotics Lab, Siddhartha Srinivasa):
- HRI 2023 Best Design Paper Award — design principles for feeding in social contexts
- Collaborator: Tyler Schrenck (paralyzed community researcher, directs assistive technology nonprofit)
- HRI 2025 out-of-lab deployment: lessons from in-the-wild use of feeding system

**Systematic review:**
- "Physically Assistive Robots" — Annual Review of Control, Robotics, and Autonomous Systems (2024, with Nanavati and Ranganeni)

## End-User Robot Programming (EUP)

Rationale: Universal robotic capabilities are infeasible. EUP expands use cases of general-purpose robots; allows customization to particular user needs and preferences. Eliminates programmer-in-the-loop for small customizations.

Key projects:
- **FLEX-SDK** (UIST 2022) — Open-source toolkit for social robot creation from two tablet screens. Nine case studies over five years.
- **ConCodeIt** — Visual concurrency programming for robots
- **University of Toronto collaboration** — EUP with human-in-the-loop adaptation to new scenarios
- **University of Wisconsin collaboration** (National Robotics Initiative grant) — Program verification/synthesis; tabletop programming environment with figurines; HRI 2023 multimodal input (path sketches + natural language)
- **IROS 2019** — Synthesizing executable programs from a single demonstration
- **RSS 2020** — Expert sketches a program with tunable holes; end-users tune variables
- **HRI 2020** — Expected divergence maximization for automatic parameter tuning

**Transfer to hardware:** EUP tools transferred to commercial robots including **Hello Robot Stretch SE2**.

Literature survey: 45 papers on end-user program specification for robots.
