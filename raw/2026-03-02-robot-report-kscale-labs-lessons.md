# 6 Lessons I Learned Watching a Robotics Startup Die from the Inside

## Company
K-Scale Labs — YC-backed humanoid robotics startup. Shut down late 2025 after failing to close Series A.

## Author background
Rui Xu — COO of K-Scale Labs. 15+ years hardware experience at Intel, Xiaomi, Lenovo, Amazon, ByteDance.

## The six lessons

1. **Large Model Chauvinism Will Get Someone Hurt**
   - AI capabilities shouldn't eliminate hardware safety fundamentals (mechanical end stops, etc.)
   - "Software fails" — models glitch at unexpected edge cases; physical consequences differ from software errors
   - 0.01% failure rates matter in hardware

2. **Oversimplified Analogies Hinder Building**
   - "Hoverboard economics," "iPhone moment" analogies mislead engineering decisions
   - "Analogies are compression algorithms" that discard critical technical details
   - Useful for fundraising, dangerous for product development

3. **Hardware Supply Chain Is Not a Task**
   - Supply chain requires sustained capabilities, not outsourcing
   - Manufacturing relationships, QC, cross-timezone logistics demand serious investment
   - "Your relationship with your CM determines whether actuators come in within tolerance"

4. **No "Commodity" Hardware Exists Yet in Robotics**
   - Each humanoid team designs custom hardware; no standardized BOM exists
   - Treating hardware as commodity diminishes engineers and their voice
   - "You can't have it both ways" — can't treat hardware as trivial and expect it to work

5. **Bad R&D Decisions Kill Faster Than Bad Luck**
   - Months on unsolved locomotion problems while fundraising windows closed
   - "Repos don't ship. Demos ship. Products ship."
   - Velocity requires convergence on working solutions, not just activity

6. **欲速則不達 (Rushing Causes Failure)**
   - Impossible deadlines force corner-cutting: unreviewed AI code, uncalibrated sensors
   - Damaged manufacturer relationships from unrealistic timelines
   - "Every skipped step comes back as a failure that costs more time than the shortcut saved"

## Core diagnosis
Overconfidence in AI + underestimation of hardware complexity = systemic optimism bias that accelerated failure.

## Final position
Xu remains bullish on embodied AI but emphasizes hardware engineering rigor equal to software sophistication.
